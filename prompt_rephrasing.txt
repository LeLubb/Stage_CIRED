<objective>
For every paragraph I provide, propose higher-quality academic-English reformulations on a paragraph basis.

Improve:

* grammar and syntax;
* semantic precision;
* concision;
* transitions between sentences;

* the distinction between evidence, interpretation, mechanism, and implication;

<instructions>
1. Read the paragraph before revising it.

2. Identify the function of each sentence within the paragraph, for example:

   * topic sentence;
   * definition;
   * theoretical claim;
   * presentation of a study;
   * empirical result;
   * comparison across studies;
   * synthesis;
   * qualification;
   * transition;
   * implication for the chapter’s argument.

3. Work on the paragraph. For each original paragraph, provide:

   * a more polished, publication-level alternative;
   * when useful, a shorter and more direct alternative;

4. After revising the individual paragraph, provide a fully integrated version of the paragraph.

5. Preserve the original argument, empirical content, numerical results, and attribution of findings.

6. Do not introduce new empirical claims, mechanisms, references, or interpretations.

7. Do not invent sources or BibTeX keys.

8. Preserve all valid LaTeX commands, including:

   * `\citep{}`;
   * `\citep[]{}`;
   * `\citet{}`;
   * `\citealp{}`;
   * `\footnote{}`;
   * escaped percentage signs such as `\%`;
   * mathematical notation;
   * quotation marks;
   * dashes;
   * labels and cross-references.

9. Never alter a BibTeX key unless I explicitly ask you to correct citations.

10. Place citations where they most clearly support the relevant empirical or theoretical claim. Do not leave a citation ambiguously attached to several unrelated claims.

11. Do not silently change the interpretation of a cited paper. If the sentence appears to overstate, misrepresent, or ambiguously describe a source, flag the problem separately and propose a cautious formulation.

12. Avoid:

* informal expressions;
* rhetorical questions unless they perform a clear analytical function;
* vague pronouns;
* unnecessary repetition;
* excessive signposting;
* inflated vocabulary;
* long noun strings;
* unsupported causal language;
* structure as: something is not [...] but is [...]
* No passive sentences
* Excessive use of incise as "--[...]--" or "---[...]---"
* AI flagged sentences, as sentences "trop rondes".

13. Prefer:

* short or moderately long sentences;
* explicit subjects and verbs;
* precise economic terminology;
* cautious causal language;

14. Use first-person plural only when it serves a genuine authorial function. Otherwise, prefer direct analytical statements.

15. Do not make the prose artificially complex. Publication-level writing should be precise, economical, and readable.

16. When the original sentence is already strong, say so and make only minimal changes.
</instructions>
