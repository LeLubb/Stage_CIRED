%LB: Petition - Fabre 25
\citet{fabre_majority_2025} ask respondents whether they would be willing to sign a petition in support of either the GCS or the NR policy\footnote{NR stands for the National Redistribution policy.}. In the United States, 51\% would sign the GCS petition and 57\% the NR petition, compared with 69\% and 65\%, respectively, in Europe. Spanish respondents report the highest willingness to sign, at 78\% for the GCS and 74\% for NR. Across all surveyed countries, the share of respondents willing to sign the GCS petition remains slightly below the share expressing support in the direct question \citep[Figure S27]{fabre_majority_2025_si}. 

%LB: List experiment - Fabre 25
\citet{fabre_majority_2025} also conduct a list experiment to assess whether social desirability inflates stated support for the GCS\footnote{\textit{Social desirability bias} occurs when respondents report opinions that present them in a favorable light.}. Respondents report only how many policies they support, while the researchers randomly vary the composition of the list and whether it includes the GCS. This design estimates tacit support for the scheme without requiring respondents to endorse it explicitly. The experiment yields an estimated support rate of 62.4\% overall, including 52.4\% in the United States and 72.4\% in Europe. Since these estimates do not fall significantly below direct stated support, the authors find no evidence of social desirability bias \citep[Table 1]{fabre_majority_2025_si}. 

%LB: conjoint analysis - Fabre 25
\citet{fabre_majority_2025} then use conjoint analyses to determine whether support for the GCS depends on its inclusion in broader policy bundles. The first analysis suggests that support for the GCS does not depend on the presence of either NR or a ``national climate policy'': ``55\% of U.S. respondents and 74\% of European ones prefer the combination of C, NR and the GCS to the combination of C and NR alone'' \citep[Figure S9]{fabre_majority_2025_si}. In a separate experiment comparing policy bundles associated with different political camps, adding the GCS to a progressive candidate's programme increased its probability of being chosen by 2.8 percentage points on average and by 11.2 points in France \citep[Supplementary Table 2]{fabre_majority_2025}.

%===============================================================================================
% - campaign effect (Gustafson et al)
%===============================================================================================

Drawing on two surveys of U.S. voters conducted in December 2018 and April 2019, \citet{gustafson_development_2019} examine how attitudes toward the Green New Deal (GND) changed after the proposal entered the national political debate. Each survey presented respondents with a description of the proposal and asked: ``How much do you support or oppose this idea?'' In December, 81\% supported the GND, including 92\% of Democrats and 64\% of Republicans. By April, the share of voters who had heard at least ``a little'' about the proposal had risen from 17\% to 59\% \citep[Figure 1]{gustafson_development_2019}. Over the same period, support declined among Republicans but remained stable among Democrats. It fell from 57\% to 32\% among conservative Republicans and from 75\% to 64\% among moderate or liberal Republicans \citep[Figure 2]{gustafson_development_2019}. In April, Republican support varied sharply with prior exposure: 85\% of those who had heard nothing about the GND supported it, compared with only 4\% of those who had heard a lot \citep[Figure 3]{gustafson_development_2019}. Among frequent Republican viewers of Fox News, support fell from 54\% to 22\%, compared with a decline from 71\% to 56\% among infrequent viewers. Since viewing habits were not randomly assigned, the authors caution that this association does not identify a causal media effect \citep[Figure 4]{gustafson_development_2019}.

%===============================================================================================
% - pluralistic ignorance
%===============================================================================================

Furthermore, \citet{fabre_public_2026} ask respondents whether they accept the Global Climate Scheme (GCS) and then elicit their second-order beliefs about the share of their compatriots who accept it. The median respondent underestimates national acceptance by 16 percentage points, while non-American respondents underestimate acceptance in the United States by 22 points \citep[Figure S30]{fabre_public_2026}. These beliefs correlate strongly with respondents' own attitudes: 51\% of GCS supporters believe that a majority of their compatriots also accept the scheme, compared with only 24\% of opponents. The author reports that ``support for the GCS reaches 72\% among the 39\% of respondents who believe that a majority in their country supports it, compared to 44\% among those who do not'' \citep[Figure S30]{fabre_public_2026}. These associations may reflect both pluralistic ignorance and a ``false consensus effect''.

%===============================================================================================
% - low salience => we should talk about "acceptance" rather than "support"
%===============================================================================================

%LB: c'est peut être pas la peine que je répète la note de bas de page n*3. Est-ce qu'il y avait une meilleure illustration du passage de 'acceptance' à 'support' qui implique 'low salience' ?
Because global redistribution attracts little spontaneous attention, \citet{fabre_public_2026} distinguishes \textit{support} from \textit{acceptance}. \textit{Support} denotes the absolute share of respondents who select ``Somewhat support'' or ``Strongly support'', whereas \textit{acceptance} measures the share of supporters among respondents who express a non-indifferent opinion. In open-ended questions asked before the survey revealed its topic, 31\% of respondents mentioned ``money'' or the ``cost of living'', while only 3.2\% explicitly referred to global inequality and ``global redistribution almost never appears as a wish'' \citep[Figure 3]{fabre_public_2026}. By contrast, when the survey presented respondents with a concrete allocation task, 87\% assigned a positive amount to public services in low-income countries \citep[Figure 4]{fabre_public_2026}. Taken together, these findings reveal broad acceptance when respondents evaluate a specific proposal but low salience in their spontaneous political concerns. They do not establish that this acceptance translates into active support capable of shaping votes or political mobilization.
